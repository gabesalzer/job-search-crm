What I'm looking for

Your standing criteria, written down before you are in the room. Every application is rated against them, and the rating is yours — nothing here calls a model or fills a number for you.

The statement

The part that does not fit an axis: why the list is what it is, what you would walk away from, what you are trying to be true of your next job. This is the half worth re-reading before a decision.

Any axis rated below {{ threshold }} disqualifies an application outright, however well it scores elsewhere. A floor beats an average: one glorious axis can otherwise pull a fatal one up out of sight, which is exactly what writing criteria down in advance is meant to stop. An unrated axis never disqualifies — unknown and failed are opposite claims.

The axes

Each application gets a {{ scale_min }}–{{ scale_max }} on every axis, and the fit score is the plain average of the ones you have rated. No weights: six hand-set weights make a number that reads more precise than the judgment behind it, and the floor already carries the "this one really matters" signal weights were reaching for.

How the pipeline scores

Fit answers a different question from the forecast. The forecast is whether they want you; this is whether you want them. The pair is the useful thing — a high fit you are unlikely to win is worth fighting for, and a low fit you are cruising through is worth closing out. Disqualified records sort last regardless of their average.
