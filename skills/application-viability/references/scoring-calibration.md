# Scoring calibration

Read this before committing to a number.

## Start from the stage, not the conversation

The single most common failure in this kind of assessment is scoring how the
*conversation* felt instead of how the *pursuit* stands. A genuinely warm,
well-connected 45-minute call at an early stage is still an early-stage
pursuit — most of them do not become offers, and the warmth of the call is
only weakly informative about which ones do.

So: anchor on the stage first, then move.

## Stage base rates

These are the starting priors — "of all applications sitting here, what
fraction end in an accepted offer?" They are rough industry-shaped defaults,
not measured facts about this user.

| Stage | Starting anchor | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Staging | 2 | Pre-application. No application has been submitted yet; the work is landing a referral or intro. Most staged roles never even become applications, so the anchor sits *below* Qualification. |
| Qualification | 5 | Is this worth pursuing at all. Almost everything here dies, most of it silently. |
| Discovery | 12 | Fit is being established in both directions. Still early; the funnel is wide here. |
| Takehome | 25 | Real investment on both sides. Selection has happened, but takehomes are also where pursuits go quiet. |
| Executive Signoff | 45 | The team wants them; the question is whether the org completes. Headcount and budget kill a real share of these. |
| Negotiation | 75 | An offer exists. Most close — but "accepted offer" still requires the user to say yes, and comp gaps are real. |
| Closed Won / Closed Lost | 100 / 0 | Terminal. Nothing to assess. |

**Replace these with the user's own numbers when available.** Their CRM
records stage history on every application, so once they have a meaningful
number of terminal outcomes, "what fraction of my Takehome-stage applications
closed won" is a real query against real data and beats any of the above. If
they mention their own conversion history, use it and say so.

## What each band is claiming

Bands, not points. The difference between 62 and 65 is noise; the difference
between 30 and 60 is a claim.

- **0–10 — effectively dead.** Explicit rejection, a role that's been pulled,
  months of silence after a clear ask, or something the user has decided they
  would decline. A 0 asserts "this is over," which is a real judgment and
  different from having no opinion.
- **11–30 — alive but unlikely.** Nothing has gone wrong, or something has,
  but the pursuit is early or has lost momentum. The honest home for most
  early-stage applications, and the band assessments most often skip past out
  of politeness.
- **31–55 — genuinely uncertain.** Real signal in both directions, or strong
  signal on one side and a large unknown on the other. Sitting here is fine
  when the evidence supports it — but check it isn't just an averaged
  non-answer.
- **56–75 — favored.** Concrete forward motion: specific next steps, named
  timelines, the employer spending real effort. Not yet an offer.
- **76–90 — likely.** An offer exists or is being explicitly prepared, and
  the user's own interest holds. Comp and terms are the remaining risk.
- **91–99 — near-certain.** Terms agreed, waiting on paper.

## Blank versus zero

Blank means "no basis to form a judgment." Zero means "this is dead." Both are
legitimate outputs and the app stores them differently on purpose. Never
default a thin input to a low number — that fills the user's calibration
history with confident readings nobody ever actually asserted, and quietly
corrupts the one thing this data is for.

Recommend blank when the input has no evaluative content at all: a scheduling
email, a one-line confirmation, a transcript that's entirely logistics.

## Common miscalibrations to check against

**Scoring the interviewer's warmth.** Interviewers are professionally warm.
It's close to free for them and near-universal, which is exactly why it
carries almost no information. See `reading-signals.md`.

**Letting one strong signal swamp the base rate.** A hiring manager saying "I
want you on my team" at Discovery is a genuinely strong signal — and still
doesn't take a 12 to 85. Managers lose headcount, panels dissent, candidates
appear. Move a lot; don't teleport.

**Reading effort as progress.** The user's effort — a great takehome, a long
thoughtful follow-up — is an input, not evidence of the employer's intent.
Weight what *they* did, not what the user did.

**Treating silence as neutral.** It isn't. Against a stated timeline that has
passed, silence is negative evidence and should move the number down.

**Averaging into the middle.** Landing at 50 because there's a positive and a
negative signal is usually an averaged non-answer rather than an assessment.
When the evidence really is balanced, say the two things are in tension and
name what would break the tie.

**Forgetting the acceptance half.** Closed Won requires the user to say yes.
Comp below their floor, a level below what they want, a team they've said
they'd dislike — these lower the score even when the employer is enthusiastic.
`Declined by me` is one of the app's real Closed Lost reasons.
